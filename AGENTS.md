# Portfolio editing rules

- Prefer evidence-led visuals throughout the website: tables for comparisons, graphs for actual measurements, and diagrams for mechanisms. Pair them with enough explanation to make the work understandable.
- Never invent statistics, confidence intervals, benchmark samples, trajectories, or performance curves. If the underlying record is unavailable, say what is known and use a qualitative table or explicitly labeled schematic instead of a numerical graph.
- Give real-data figures a clear model/hardware/workload, date or experiment scope, units, baseline, and measurement boundary. Do not combine results from different configurations into a fabricated headline. Keep source-to-figure provenance in the task evidence; publish only appropriate sanitized aggregates.
- Distinguish observed results, historical reported measurements, planned work, and unverified integration. A benchmark is not a production outcome or clinical validation.
- Remove synthetic animations that could be mistaken for recorded numerical results. Mechanism illustrations must be labeled as illustrations and must not imply measured traces.
- Do not tout commit counts, lines of code, database size, or counts of launch services.
- Condense short entries into one coherent reading page. Keep section navigation for substantial case studies. Use the same single-page policy for the IDE explorer, reading view, file order, and dedicated detail views; keep older deep links usable.
- Keep PRSM's public description high level and protect proprietary implementation details. Keep BeHuman public-safe: no patient/client information, internal links, confidential measurements, or invented clinical outcomes. The patient-chart-analysis case may explain the verified approach and qualitative results in greater depth.
- Keep independent research and personal projects separate from BeHuman work. Do not imply Anthropic employment or endorsement.
- BeHuman: Product Owner from June 2026. Formulatrix ends June 2026. Preserve other verified dates.
- Preserve the existing design and unrelated edits. Keep credentials, environment values, private personal fields, runtime stores, raw research notes, and private startup illustrations outside the public Git repository.
