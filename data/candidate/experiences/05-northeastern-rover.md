## Bullets

- Worked with large team of Northeastern students to design a 50kg rover for the University Rover Challenge.

## Detail

### 6-DOF arm

Led the arm sub-team designing a 6-DOF manipulator for the University Rover Challenge — science sample retrieval, button-push servicing on a mock equipment panel, and tele-operated object manipulation. Six degrees of freedom were the minimum to reach arbitrary pose targets inside the science payload's working volume while keeping the wrist clear of the chassis.

![6-DOF arm — isometric CAD](/media/experiences/neu-rover/arm-cad.jpg)

![Structural FEA on the arm — load cases for full extension and gripper-side payload](/media/experiences/neu-rover/arm-analysis.png)

The end-effector went through several gripper iterations to balance the competing requirements of field manipulation.

| Requirement | Design question |
| --- | --- |
| Grip force | Can the gripper hold the objects required by the task? |
| Sample-tube compatibility | Can it handle the science payload's tubes? |
| Field serviceability | Can the team maintain it with the tooling available in Utah? |

The CAD, analysis, and prototype images show the design work; the competition placing is a reported team outcome.

### Mechanical co-lead

Owned subsystem integration across arm, drivetrain, science payload, and chassis on a 50 kg field-serviceable rover, tele-operable over a multi-mile competition course. Cross-disciplinary student team — mechanical, electrical, and software — with the integration interfaces (mounting, harness routing, thermal, mass budget) cutting across all three.
