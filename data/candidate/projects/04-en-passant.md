# En Passant — Team Lead, Autonomous Chessboard

## Bullets

- Led a 5-person multidisciplinary capstone-style team (ME / EE / CS, Jira-tracked) building an autonomous chessboard with under-board piece movement and per-square piece identification + localization. **Python, C (ESP-IDF), Arduino, and C++**.
- **Motion system**: CoreXY belt-driven gantry with NEMA 11 + NEMA 8 (PG90 planetary) steppers, A3040 servo-arm Z lift end-effector, magnetic piece-drag drive under a non-magnetic playing surface; **A\* path planner with 8-connectivity** routing piece moves around occupied squares on an inflated `17 × 19` board grid (one inflated cell per square edge for routing margin); UCI move strings translated to gantry waypoints via `motion_planner.py`.
- **Capacitive sensor array**: custom KiCad-designed sensor PCB with **80 capacitive channels** (5 ESP32 hardware-touch lines × 16:1 analog mux × 2 row/column groups) read via ESP32 IDF (`touch_pad_read_raw_data`, IIR filter, ~17 Hz full-board scan); per-piece passive copper-pad **PCB tags with type-coded capacitance** (12 tag strengths logarithmically spaced); host-side localization by **outer-product cross-talk reconstruction** of row × column readings, with **per-pixel Kalman filtering** + **GMM peak fitting** (BIC model selection) to extract sub-pixel piece centers at ~2.5 mm accuracy.
- **ML for piece localization**: dual Keras + PyTorch (MPS-aware) regression-CNN stack outputting up to 32 (x, y) piece coordinates per frame, with a **custom permutation-invariant Hungarian-style loss** matching predictions to ground-truth tags within type-counted groups (8 pawns, 2 knights, 2 bishops, 2 rooks, 1 queen, 1 king per side). Custom `ContrastConv2d` Laplacian-edge layer on the PyTorch side. Trained on a 472 MB synthetic dataset generated by a multiprocessing pipeline I built; 300 epochs, 30 saved checkpoints.
- **Online-play integration**: bidirectional Lichess API integration via `berserk` — local moves stream to Lichess and remote-opponent moves stream back into the gantry queue. No local chess engine; Lichess is the legality / rule authority.
- **Locomotion + sensing roadmap (Sept–Nov 2024 R&D)**: planned a three-stage evolution — shipped **stage 1** (CoreXY gantry + analog capacitive array, two separate hardware stacks for motion vs. ID) → **stage 2** linear motors with absolute encoders (drop the belt-and-pulley height stack, kill backlash, keep the capacitive sensing array) → **stage 3** fully solid-state PCB-stator coil array with **dual-use coils** doing locomotion *and* RFID-style identification on the same hardware. R&D effort during the pivot focused on stage 3: forked a public KiCad coil-generator plugin (6-circular / 6-wedge / 12-wedge stator topologies), simulated coil-array fields and per-coil forces using a Biot–Savart Python implementation, designed the time-multiplexed actuate/sense cycle, characterized coil thermals on real hardware with an NTC thermistor + Steinhart–Hart calibration. PCB layout was in-progress when the project paused as Sovlscan scaled.

## Detail

Capstone-style project to make remote online chess feel physical: the opponent's moves replay on a real board with real pieces, and the board reads back which piece sits where without cameras. I led a five-person ME / EE / CS team and ran the architecture, sensing stack, and EM-drive R&D personally.

### Motion system

The motion stack has a three-stage roadmap: each stage trades complexity for thinness and integration, ending at a single physical layer that does both motion and sensing.

#### Stage 1 — CoreXY gantry + magnetic cart (shipped)

A CoreXY gantry below a non-magnetic playing surface drags ferrous-base pieces with a magnetic cart. NEMA 11 / NEMA 8 (PG90 planetary) steppers on GT2 belts run X / Y; an A3040 micro-servo on the cart performs Z-lift to engage and disengage the magnet between moves. UCI move strings are translated to gantry waypoints by `motion_planner.py`, which feeds the [path planner](#path-planner-and-lichess-integration) for collision-aware routing.

![CoreXY gantry — belt-driven X/Y under the playing surface](/media/projects/en-passant/corexy.mov)

#### Stage 2 — Linear motors with absolute encoders (planned)

Replace the belt-driven gantry with **linear motors driving the cart on each axis, with absolute optical encoders for closed-loop position**. The goal is thinness: drop the belt + pulley + idler stack, kill backlash, and shrink the box height under the playing surface. Sensing stays on the [capacitive array](#sensing-modalities-and-time-multiplexing); this stage modernizes the motion path without re-architecting perception.

#### Stage 3 — Solid-state coil array (R&D vision)

No moving cart at all. A PCB-stator coil array under the entire board drives ferrous-base pieces via switched fields, and the same coils — time-multiplexed between drive and probe — handle identification and localization. One PCB layer does locomotion *and* perception. The rest of this section covers the stage-3 hardware exploration; the [sensing modalities](#sensing-modalities-and-time-multiplexing) and [coil hardware](#coil-hardware-signal-processing-and-tradeoffs) sections cover the sensing side and the engineering tradeoffs.

#### Two modes per coil

Stage 3's central conceptual move: stop treating "move pieces" and "identify pieces" as two separate hardware stacks. The same coils that drive the cart are also passive sensors when the drive is briefly disabled.

- **Actuation mode (drive)** — coil is energized with controlled current; generates the field that moves or holds the magnetic element below.
- **Sensing mode (probe)** — coil is excited with a small signal or briefly pulsed; the system measures the electrical response (impedance, decay, induced voltage). Nearby tags or materials modulate the coil's electromagnetic characteristics, so the coil's electrical behavior itself becomes the readout.

:::callout
**Key insight.** Every actuator is also a sensor. The coil geometry that maximizes lateral force on a magnetic cart is also the geometry that maximizes flux linkage with tag resonators above the board. One PCB, one driver topology — locomotion *and* perception out of the same hardware.
:::

### Sensing modalities and time-multiplexing

The board reads back where each piece sits and what type it is. Stage 1 ships a passive analog capacitive array; stage 3 unifies sensing into the same coil array that drives motion, with three different read modes interleaved between actuation phases.

#### Stage 1 — Capacitive sensing array (shipped)

A custom KiCad sensor PCB sits under the board face. ESP32 hardware-touch lines fan out through 16:1 analog muxes into row/column groups, and each piece carries a passive copper-pad tag whose trace area encodes its type. The host reconstructs an 80×80 cross-talk grid from the row/column readings.

:::stats
- 80 capacitive channels · 5 ESP32 touch lines × 16:1 mux × 2 row/col
- 12 tag strengths · logarithmically spaced, type-coded per piece
- ~2.5 mm accuracy · sub-pixel piece-center extraction
- ~17 Hz · full-board scan rate
:::

#### Stage 3 — Coils as sensors (physical principle)

A coil is an RLC system — resistance **R**, inductance **L**, parasitic capacitance **C** — and nearby objects perturb all three. The coupling that does work on a piece also encodes information about it.

- **Conductive bodies** induce eddy currents → damping rises (R↑) and effective inductance shifts.
- **Resonant LC tags** absorb energy at their tuned frequency and re-radiate a detectable signature.
- **Magnetic / permeable materials** change L through permeability shifts; the same ferrous element being pushed becomes a dielectric perturbation that the coil can read.

Three different ways to read this perturbation, each useful for a different sub-problem.

#### Ring-down

Drive the coil with a short pulse, abruptly stop driving, observe the voltage decay. Extract the decay time constant and (if underdamped) the oscillation frequency. Sensitive to nearby conductive bodies and to resonant tags above the coil.

#### Frequency sweep / impedance

Inject a sinusoidal excitation across a frequency band and measure amplitude / phase response. Resonant peaks identify the tag; impedance shifts encode proximity and position.

#### Backscatter detection

The tag dynamically modulates its own load. The driving coil sees small variations in current / voltage that decode into a digital ID — the same protocol family as commodity RFID backscatter.

#### Time-multiplexed cycle

A single coil can't simultaneously push amps and read millivolts, so the control loop interleaves four phases:

1. **Actuate** at high current.
2. **Disable drive** — tri-state the output stage into a high-impedance state.
3. **Sense** in a low-energy probing window.
4. **Resume actuation**.

The sense window has to be short relative to the cart's motion dynamics (so locomotion still feels continuous) but long enough for coil ringing to settle before the ADC samples.

#### Spatial encoding across multiple coils

Each coil reads a localized region, and overlapping fields produce a spatial signature across the array. Strongest-coupling coil estimates position; multi-coil readout differentiates simultaneous tags and tracks pieces as they move between cells.

#### Research context

The stage-3 approach sits at the intersection of magnetic manipulation systems, near-field RFID, impedance spectroscopy, and sensorless sensing in electromechanical systems. Closest neighbors: inductive metal detection, wireless-power-transfer coupling analysis, and RFID backscatter communication.

### ML piece localization

The cross-talk grid feeds a Kalman noise filter and a regression CNN that predicts up to 32 piece coordinates per frame. A custom permutation-invariant Hungarian-style loss matches predictions to ground truth within type-counted groups, so identical pieces (8 pawns, etc.) are interchangeable. Trained on 472 MB of synthetic data; PyTorch and Keras stacks both ran, with ~2.5 mm sub-pixel accuracy on simulated input.

### Identification strategy and closed-loop control

#### Encoding identity per piece

Each piece-tag is designed with a unique electromagnetic signature. Three encoding axes are available, and the design space combines them:

- **Resonant-frequency encoding** — each tag tuned to a different LC frequency.
- **Multi-resonant tags** — multiple peaks per tag → much higher encoding capacity.
- **Damping-based encoding** — different Q-factors or loss profiles per piece type.

#### Detection pipeline

1. Collect response from one or more coils across the array.
2. Extract features — peak frequencies, amplitude response, decay constants.
3. Classify the feature vector against the known per-piece signature library.

#### Closed-loop motion + sensing

With identity *and* position emerging from the same coils, the control loop becomes:

- **Sense** — detect tag identity and current cell.
- **Plan** — compute the force vector that moves the piece toward the target cell.
- **Actuate** — energize the appropriate coil(s) for that vector.
- **Re-sense** — confirm motion, repeat until at goal.

#### Example behaviors enabled

- Move a specific piece toward a target square without any external tracker.
- Identify the piece *before* manipulating it (different control gains for a knight than for a rook).
- Adapt control parameters at runtime based on the sensed signature — e.g., damping a heavier piece more aggressively.

### Coil hardware, signal processing, and tradeoffs

![Coil hardware — drive / sense isolation and signal flow](/media/projects/en-passant/en-passant-coil-hardware.mp4)

#### Hardware requirements

- **Coil driver** — bidirectional or H-bridge stage for actuation, with fast enable / disable so the output can be tri-stated for the sense window.
- **Sensing front-end** — high-impedance measurement path, ADC with enough resolution to read the post-pulse decay, optional analog gain and filtering.
- **Switching** — analog switches or tri-state drivers to isolate the high-impedance sensing node from the high-current power stage.

#### Signal processing

- **Feature extraction** — FFT for frequency response, curve fitting for decay constants, peak detection for resonance ID.
- **Estimation** — Bayesian filtering across multiple coil readings for piece position; classification models for tag identity.
- **ML extensions** — learn the mapping from raw coil waveforms directly to identity + position. More robust to environmental drift than hand-tuned features.

#### Challenges

- **Dynamic range** — actuation currents are amps, sensing signals are millivolts. Demands careful isolation and fast settling between phases.
- **Ringing & transients** — switching the coil produces large voltage spikes; the front-end has to wait for stabilization before sensing.
- **Thermal effects** — coil resistance drifts with temperature, shifting baseline impedance. Needs ongoing recalibration during long runs.
- **Environmental sensitivity** — nearby metal structures (mounts, fasteners, the gantry frame) distort the field; calibration or adaptive models are required.
- **Multiplexing complexity** — many coils sharing one sensing front-end means careful timing coordination across the array.

#### Advantages and limitations

**Advantages.** No separate RFID antenna stack. Perfect spatial alignment between actuation and sensing — the field doing the work is the field doing the reading. Reduced hardware complexity and tightly coupled perception-action loops.

**Limitations.** Sensing bandwidth is bounded by the time-multiplexing cycle. Sensitivity is lower than a dedicated RF system would deliver. Performance depends heavily on coil geometry and on the surrounding environment.
